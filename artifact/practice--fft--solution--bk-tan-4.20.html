The tone is at f=2000f=2000f=2000 Hz, i.e. 20008000=14\frac{2000}{8000}=\frac1480002000​=41​ cycle per sample, or one cycle every 4 samples.

When does leakage occur? The DFT treats the NNN samples as one period of a periodic signal. If the record holds a whole number of cycles, this periodic extension is a perfect sinusoid, and the tone falls exactly on a bin. Otherwise the extension has a jump, and energy spreads ("leaks") into every bin.

(a) N=100N=100N=100:

  • Δf=8000100=80\Delta f=\frac{8000}{100}=80Δf=1008000​=80 Hz.
  • The tone sits at 200080=25\frac{2000}{80}=25802000​=25, exactly bin 25.
  • The record holds 1004=25\frac{100}{4}=254100​=25 whole cycles.
  • No leakage: the amplitude spectrum shows a single clean line at 2000 Hz.

(b) N=73N=73N=73:

  • Δf=800073≈109.6\Delta f=\frac{8000}{73}\approx109.6Δf=738000​≈109.6 Hz.
  • The tone sits at 2000109.6≈18.25\frac{2000}{109.6}\approx18.25109.62000​≈18.25, between bins 18 and 19.
  • The record holds 18.2518.2518.25 cycles, not a whole number.
  • Leakage occurs: the peak is spread over neighbouring bins and lowered, and small values appear everywhere.

In practice the signal frequency is unknown, so leakage is the normal case. That is why window functions (Hamming, Hann) are applied before the FFT.
