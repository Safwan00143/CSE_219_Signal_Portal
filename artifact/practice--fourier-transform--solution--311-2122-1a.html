The four pictures.

  1. The single pulse in time.
  2. Its continuous sinc spectrum.
  3. The pulse train in time.
  4. A line spectrum: impulses at f=n/(2B)f=n/(2B)f=n/(2B) whose heights follow the same sinc envelope, scaled by 1/T01/T_01/T0​.

Correlation.

  • Periodic in time ⇔ discrete in frequency. The Fourier-series lines are samples of the single pulse's transform: Dn=1T0G(nf0)D_n=\frac1{T_0}G(nf_0)Dn​=T0​1​G(nf0​).
  • The envelope, and therefore the essential bandwidth (about 1/B1/B1/B), depends only on the pulse width, not on whether the pulse repeats. Repetition only decides where we sample the envelope, at spacing f0=1/T0f_0=1/T_0f0​=1/T0​.
  • Even harmonics vanish here because they land exactly on the sinc's zeros at multiples of 1/B1/B1/B.
  • As T0→∞T_0\to\inftyT0​→∞ the lines merge into the continuous spectrum. That is how the Fourier transform arises from the Fourier series.
