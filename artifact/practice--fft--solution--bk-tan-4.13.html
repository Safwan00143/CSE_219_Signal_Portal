The resolution of an NNN-point DFT is Δf=fsN\Delta f=\frac{f_s}{N}Δf=Nfs​​. We need

2000N<0.5 ⇒ N>4000.\frac{2000}{N}<0.5\ \Rightarrow\ N>4000.N2000​<0.5 ⇒ N>4000.

A radix-2 FFT needs NNN to be a power of 2. The smallest power of 2 above 4000 is

N=212=4096.\begin{aligned}&N\\[6pt]&= 2^{12}\\[6pt]&= 4096.\end{aligned}​N=212=4096.​

The actual resolution is

Δf=20004096=0.488 Hz<0.5 Hz. ✓\begin{aligned}&\Delta f\\[6pt]&= \frac{2000}{4096}\\[6pt]&= 0.488\ \text{Hz}<0.5\ \text{Hz}.\ ✓\end{aligned}​Δf=40962000​=0.488 Hz<0.5 Hz. ✓​

This corresponds to 40962000=2.048\frac{4096}{2000}=2.04820004096​=2.048 s of data. If only 4000 samples had been recorded, we would append 96 zeros to reach 4096. The FFT would run, but the true resolution is set by the 2-second record, not by the padding.
