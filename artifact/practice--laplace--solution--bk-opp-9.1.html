The factor e−jωte^{-j\omega t}e−jωt has magnitude 1, so convergence depends only on the real exponential. Look at what happens at each infinite end:

  • an integrand e−cte^{-ct}e−ct dies at t→+∞t\to+\inftyt→+∞ when c>0c>0c>0;
  • it dies at t→−∞t\to-\inftyt→−∞ when c<0c<0c<0.

(a) e−(5+σ)te^{-(5+\sigma)t}e−(5+σ)t on (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞) needs 5+σ>05+\sigma>05+σ>0:  σ>−5\ \sigma>-5 σ>−5.

(b) The same exponential on (−∞,0)(-\infty,0)(−∞,0) needs 5+σ<05+\sigma<05+σ<0:  σ<−5\ \sigma<-5 σ<−5.

(c) A finite interval with a bounded integrand: converges for all σ\sigmaσ.

(d) It needs both (a) and (b) at once, σ>−5\sigma>-5σ>−5 and σ<−5\sigma<-5σ<−5: no value of σ\sigmaσ works.

(e) For t>0t>0t>0 we need σ>−5\sigma>-5σ>−5. For t<0t<0t<0 the integrand is e(5−σ)te^{(5-\sigma)t}e(5−σ)t, which needs σ<5\sigma<5σ<5. So  −5<σ<5\ -5<\sigma<5 −5<σ<5.

(f) Only the t<0t<0t<0 half:  σ<5\ \sigma<5 σ<5.

These are exactly the ROC rules:

  • right-sided signals have ROCs to the right of a line;
  • left-sided signals have ROCs to the left;
  • two-sided signals have a strip;
  • finite-duration signals have the whole plane.
