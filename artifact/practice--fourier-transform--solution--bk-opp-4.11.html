Work in the frequency domain, where convolution becomes multiplication.

x(3t)↔13X(jω3),h(3t)↔13H(jω3).x(3t)\leftrightarrow\tfrac13X\left(\tfrac{j\omega}3\right),\qquad h(3t)\leftrightarrow\tfrac13H\left(\tfrac{j\omega}3\right).x(3t)↔31​X(3jω​),h(3t)↔31​H(3jω​).

So

G(jω)=19X(jω3)H(jω3)=19Y(jω3).\begin{aligned}&G(j\omega)\\[6pt]&= \tfrac19X\left(\tfrac{j\omega}3\right)H\left(\tfrac{j\omega}3\right)\\[6pt]&= \tfrac19Y\left(\tfrac{j\omega}3\right).\end{aligned}​G(jω)=91​X(3jω​)H(3jω​)=91​Y(3jω​).​

Compare with the transform of y(3t)y(3t)y(3t), which is 13Y(jω3)\frac13Y\left(\frac{j\omega}3\right)31​Y(3jω​). Then

G(jω)=13⋅[13Y(jω3)] ⇒ g(t)=13 y(3t).\begin{aligned}&G(j\omega)=\tfrac13\cdot\left[\tfrac13Y\left(\tfrac{j\omega}3\right)\right]\ \\[6pt]&\Rightarrow \ g(t)=\tfrac13\,y(3t).\end{aligned}​G(jω)=31​⋅[31​Y(3jω​)] ⇒ g(t)=31​y(3t).​

A=13,B=3\boxed{A=\tfrac13,\quad B=3}A=31​,B=3​

Intuition: compressing both signals by 3 compresses their convolution by 3. The convolution integral also runs over a variable that has been compressed, which scales its area by 13\frac1331​. A quick check: two unit pulses of width 1 convolve to a triangle of height 1. Pulses of width 13\frac1331​ give a triangle of height 13\frac1331​.
