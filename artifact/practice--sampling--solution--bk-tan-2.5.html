(a) Each tone f0f_0f0​ produces lines at nfs±f0nf_s\pm f_0nfs​±f0​:

  • 2500 Hz (amplitude 2.5): 2.5, 5.5, 10.5, 13.5, 18.5 kHz.

(b) The 4 kHz low-pass filter keeps only the lines below 4 kHz: 2500 Hz (true) and 3500 Hz (an image of 4500 Hz). The recovered signal is

x^(t)=5cos⁡(2π×2500t)+2cos⁡(2π×3500t).\hat x(t)=5\cos(2\pi\times2500t)+2\cos(2\pi\times3500t).x^(t)=5cos(2π×2500t)+2cos(2π×3500t).

(c) The 4500 Hz component exceeds the folding frequency fs2=4000\frac{f_s}{2}=40002fs​​=4000 Hz. It "folds" back to

falias=fs−4500=3500 Hz.\begin{aligned}&f_{\text{alias}}\\[6pt]&= f_s-4500\\[6pt]&= 3500\ \text{Hz}.\end{aligned}​falias​=fs​−4500=3500 Hz.​

So the aliasing noise is at 3500 Hz. It cannot be removed after sampling, because it sits inside the signal band. It must be prevented by an anti-aliasing filter before the sampler.
