Factor everything:

  • Numerator: 1−14z−2=(1−12z−1)(1+12z−1)1-\frac14z^{-2}=\left(1-\frac12z^{-1}\right)\left(1+\frac12z^{-1}\right)1−41​z−2=(1−21​z−1)(1+21​z−1), so zeros at z=±12z=\pm\frac12z=±21​.
  • 1+14z−2=01+\frac14z^{-2}=01+41​z−2=0 gives z2=−14z^2=-\frac14z2=−41​, so poles at z=±j2z=\pm\frac j2z=±2j​.
  • 1+54z−1+38z−2=(1+12z−1)(1+34z−1)1+\frac54z^{-1}+\frac38z^{-2}=\left(1+\frac12z^{-1}\right)\left(1+\frac34z^{-1}\right)1+45​z−1+83​z−2=(1+21​z−1)(1+43​z−1), so poles at z=−12z=-\frac12z=−21​ and z=−34z=-\frac34z=−43​.

The factor (1+12z−1)\left(1+\frac12z^{-1}\right)(1+21​z−1) cancels, so the pole at −12-\frac12−21​ disappears. The remaining poles are ±j2\pm\frac j2±2j​ (radius 12\frac1221​) and −34-\frac34−43​ (radius 34\frac3443​).

ROCs are annuli bounded by pole radii. Two distinct radii give 3 possible ROCs:

∣z∣<12,12<∣z∣<34,∣z∣>34.|z|<\tfrac12,\qquad\tfrac12<|z|<\tfrac34,\qquad|z|>\tfrac34.∣z∣<21​,21​<∣z∣<43​,∣z∣>43​.

Even without the cancellation the answer would be 3, because −12-\frac12−21​ has the same radius as ±j2\pm\frac j2±2j​. It is still good practice to cancel first.
